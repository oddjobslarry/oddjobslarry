What Should You Do If Your Sump Pump Fails?

If your sump pump fails, shut off power to the pit if standing water is near electrical components, then begin removing water immediately with a wet vac or by bucket if needed. Sump pumps typically fail from power outages, motor burnout, clogged discharge lines, or heavy rainfall that overwhelms pump capacity. Call a restoration company right away. Mold can begin growing within 24-48 hours of standing water, so fast extraction and drying matters more than almost anything else.

Local Risk Factors

Why Basement Flooding Is Common in Kenosha

Kenosha's location on Lake Michigan means the ground stays saturated with moisture for much of the year, and heavy thunderstorms can drop inches of rain in a matter of hours. Add in spring snowmelt, which sends a sudden surge of groundwater toward foundations every year, and it's easy to see why sump pumps here work harder than almost anywhere else in the state.

Kenosha's housing stock, much of it built between the 1920s and 1970s, adds another layer of risk: aging foundations are more prone to seepage, older sump systems often lack modern battery backups, and high groundwater tables in low-lying areas near the lakefront put constant pressure on basement walls and floors.

A sump pump is only rated to move a certain volume of water per hour. When inflow from heavy rainfall or snowmelt exceeds that capacity, or when water finds another path into the basement entirely (through a foundation crack, window well, or floor drain), flooding happens even with a fully functional pump. Root Causes

Common Causes of Sump Pump Failure

Understanding why sump pumps fail is the first step toward preventing the next flood.

⚡

Power Outage

Battery backup missing. A pump can't run without power, and storms are the most common cause of both flooding and outages at the same time.

⚙️

Pump Failure

Motor burnout from age or overuse leaves the system unable to move water even when power is available.

🎚️

Float Switch Failure

Pump never activates. A stuck or misaligned float switch means the pump doesn't know water has entered the pit.

🚧

Clogged Discharge Line

Water cannot exit the home. Debris, sediment, or a nesting animal can block the line even when the pump itself works fine.

🧊

Frozen Discharge Pipe

Common during Wisconsin winters, a frozen discharge line backs water up into the pit with nowhere to go.

🌧️

Heavy Storms

Pump overwhelmed by incoming water when rainfall volume exceeds what a single pump can handle.

Prevention tip: Battery backup systems are one of the single most effective ways to prevent basement flooding during storms and power outages, since they keep the pump running exactly when it's needed most.

Technical Standards

Professional Basement Drying After Sump Pump Failure

Not all basement water is the same, and how we dry it depends on its source. We classify every job according to IICRC S500 standards, the industry reference for water damage restoration, and take moisture readings throughout the process to confirm the structure is actually dry, not just visibly dry.

Category 1 Water

Clean water from a sump pump or supply line, the least contaminated but still capable of causing mold if not dried quickly.

Category 2 Water

Grey water with some contamination, such as water that's been sitting or has picked up materials from flooring or furniture.

Category 3 Water

Grossly contaminated water, including groundwater or anything mixed with sewage, requiring stricter containment and disposal.

Our process includes drywall drying, subfloor drying, and hidden moisture detection behind walls and under flooring, since moisture that isn't fully addressed becomes the source of mold and structural problems weeks later.

Mold Can Begin Growing Within 24-48 Hours

Wet drywall, wet insulation, and soaked carpet are all ideal conditions for mold, and colonization can start in as little as a day or two after a sump pump failure. Musty odors are often the first sign that hidden moisture is trapped somewhere it can't be seen.

If your basement has flooded and you're noticing a persistent musty smell even after the visible water is gone, it's worth a closer look. Our mold remediation after water damage team can inspect for hidden moisture and stop growth before it spreads further into the structure.

Insurance Help

Insurance Coverage for Basement Flooding

Basement flooding coverage depends heavily on the source of the water. A sudden sump pump failure is often covered under a standard homeowners policy, while flooding caused by sewer backup or surface water typically requires a separate sewer backup rider, an add-on many Kenosha policies don't include by default.

Prevention

How to Prevent Future Basement Flooding

Homeowners in local Kenosha forums and community groups consistently point to the same lesson after a flood: fixing drainage and grading problems outside the home matters just as much as the pump itself.

  • Battery backup systems Keep your pump running through power outages, the single highest-impact upgrade you can make.
  • Leak monitoring Water sensors near the sump pit alert you to rising water before it becomes a full flood.
  • Annual maintenance A yearly pump test and pit cleaning catches float switch and motor issues before storm season.
  • Discharge line inspections Checking for clogs and freeze risk keeps water actually leaving the home once pumped.
  • Gutter maintenance Clean gutters and properly extended downspouts keep roof water away from the foundation.
  • Proper grading Soil that slopes away from the foundation prevents surface water from pooling against basement walls.
