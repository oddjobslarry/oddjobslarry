What Should You Do After Storm or Flood Damage?

After storm or flood damage, ensure personal safety first and avoid contact with floodwater, which can contain sewage, chemicals, debris, and other contaminants. Stay away from downed power lines and electrical hazards, document all damage with photos and video, and begin emergency water removal immediately. Fast drying reduces the risk of mold growth, which can start within 24–48 hours. Flooding is one of Wisconsin's most common and costly natural disasters, so contact restoration professionals right away rather than waiting.

Local Risk Factors

Why Storm & Flood Damage Is Common in Kenosha

Kenosha's location on Lake Michigan means storm systems can drop heavy rain in a short window, the kind of downpour that overwhelms storm drains, saturates already-wet ground, and pushes water toward foundations across the city. Combine that with spring thaw, when melting snow adds a sudden surge of groundwater, and Kenosha sees flash flooding, basement flooding, and foundation seepage far more often than inland communities without lakefront weather patterns.

Stormwater systems built decades ago weren't designed for today's rainfall intensity, and when they overload, water backs up into yards, window wells, and basements before homeowners even realize a storm has become a flood. Wisconsin experiences recurring severe weather each year, and storm-related property damage remains one of the most frequent insurance claims filed by Kenosha homeowners.

Technical Standards

Understanding Flood Water Contamination

Floodwater is frequently treated as Category 3 contamination because it may contain sewage, chemicals, and biological contaminants, and how it's classified determines how the cleanup is handled under IICRC S500 standards.

Category 1 Water

Clean water from a broken supply line or rainwater with no contact with soil or contaminants. Still requires fast extraction to prevent mold.

Category 2 Water

Grey water containing some contamination, such as floodwater that has picked up chemicals or organic material as it travels.

Category 3 Water

Grossly contaminated black water, including sewage-mixed floodwater, groundwater, and storm surge. Requires biohazard-level PPE and containment.

Mold Prevention

Prevent Mold Growth After Flood Damage

Mold growth can begin within 24–48 hours of flood exposure, and it doesn't need standing water to take hold. Hidden moisture trapped inside wet drywall, wet insulation, and subfloor contamination is often where mold starts, long before it becomes visible or produces a noticeable odor.

Flood cleanup discussions consistently stress rapid drying to reduce mold growth risk, which is why we begin structural drying and moisture monitoring as soon as extraction starts rather than waiting until debris removal is finished.

If your property has flooded and you're noticing a musty smell even after the visible water is gone, our mold remediation after water damage team can inspect for hidden moisture before it spreads further into the structure.

Where Mold Hides After a Flood

  • Behind wet drywall and baseboards
  • Inside soaked insulation
  • Under carpet padding and subflooring
  • Inside HVAC ductwork exposed to floodwater
  • Within crawl spaces and unfinished basements

How to Avoid Storm Damage Restoration Scams

After a major storm, transient "storm chaser" crews often go door-to-door in Kenosha neighborhoods offering fast repairs. Wisconsin consumer protection guidance warns homeowners to be cautious of unfamiliar crews with no local presence, especially in the days right after a storm.

  • Storm chasers and door-to-door contractors with no local address or references
  • Requests for full payment upfront before any work begins
  • Anyone offering to waive or pay your insurance deductible, a known insurance-fraud red flag
  • Pressure to sign an assignment of benefits (AOB) on the spot
  • No written estimate or scope of work before work starts
