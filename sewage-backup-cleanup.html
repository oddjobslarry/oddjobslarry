What Should You Do After a Sewage Backup?

If you experience a sewage backup, leave the contaminated area immediately and keep children and pets away. Avoid touching or walking through affected water, and turn off your HVAC system if it is safe to do so, to stop airborne contamination from spreading. Then call a professional sewage cleanup company right away. Contamination Classification

Understanding Category 3 Water Damage

Every water damage job in Kenosha is classified by contamination level under the IICRC S500 standard. Sewage backups always fall into the most severe category.

Category 1

Clean Water

Originates from a sanitary source such as a supply line or appliance. Poses little to no health risk if addressed quickly, but can escalate to a higher category over time.

Category 2

Grey Water

Contains some contamination, such as water from a washing machine or dishwasher overflow. Can cause illness if ingested or if contact continues untreated.

Category 3

Black Water

Grossly contaminated water from sewage backups, sewer laterals, or floodwater. Classified as a biohazard cleanup requiring containment, negative air filtration, and specialized removal procedures.

Sewage backups are always Category 3, the highest contamination level. This water may carry pathogens and requires strict containment before any extraction begins.
Why It Happens Here

Common Causes of Sewage Backups in Kenosha Homes

Kenosha's older housing stock and lakefront storm patterns make basement sewer backup cleanup one of our most common emergency sewage removal calls. Heavy rainfall off Lake Michigan and spring thaw conditions regularly overwhelm municipal storm sewer systems, pushing wastewater back into basements through floor drains, and a simple toilet overflow cleanup can quickly turn into a full sewer backup cleanup job once contamination spreads.

  • 🌧️
    Heavy Rainfall & Storm Sewer Overload: Intense Lake Michigan storms can overwhelm the municipal sewer system faster than it can drain.
  • 🌳
    Tree Root Intrusion: Mature trees in older neighborhoods send roots into aging clay sewer laterals, causing blockages and backups.
  • 🏚️
    Aging Sewer Lines: Kenosha's 1920s to 1970s housing stock often still runs on original clay or cast iron laterals prone to cracking and collapse.
  • 🚿
    Basement Floor Drains: The lowest point of entry in a home, floor drains are typically the first place backed up sewage appears.
  • 🏙️
    Municipal Sewer Backups: Capacity issues in the city's combined or aging sewer infrastructure can push wastewater into connected homes during peak flow events.

Why Sewage Cleanup Is a Serious Health Hazard

Category 3 water may contain pathogens capable of causing serious illness, which is why sewage cleanup is never a DIY project.

E. coli
Salmonella
Norovirus
Hepatitis A
Parasites
Airborne Contamination: Disturbed sewage can release aerosolized particles that spread pathogens beyond the immediate area of the backup.
Cross Contamination: Contaminated water can spread through foot traffic, cleaning tools, and HVAC systems to unaffected rooms.
Respiratory Irritation: Fumes and airborne particles from Category 3 water can trigger respiratory symptoms, especially in children and those with asthma.
Skin Exposure Risks: Direct contact with sewage water can cause infection, rashes, and illness without proper protective equipment.
Contaminated Materials

Materials Often Removed After a Sewage Backup

Porous materials exposed to Category 3 water often cannot be safely restored and must be removed as part of the cleanup process.

🧵

Carpet & Padding

Absorbs contamination quickly and cannot be sanitized to a safe standard.

🧱

Drywall

Wicks contaminated water upward, often requiring removal well above the visible waterline.

🧊

Insulation

Traps moisture and pathogens deep inside wall and floor cavities.

🛋️

Upholstered Furniture

Fabric and cushioning absorb contamination that cannot be fully removed by cleaning.

🪵

Particle Board

Swells and breaks down when exposed to contaminated water, and cannot be sanitized.

📦

Cardboard & Paper Goods

Highly porous and among the fastest materials to become unsalvageable.

Non-porous materials such as tile, glass, and solid wood can typically be cleaned and sanitized, while porous materials exposed to sewage often cannot be safely restored.

Secondary Damage

Prevent Mold Growth After a Sewage Backup

Mold can begin colonizing within 24 to 48 hours of a sewage backup if moisture is not fully extracted. Hidden moisture behind walls, wet insulation, and subfloor contamination are the most common places mold takes hold after a Category 3 event, often long before it becomes visible.

Fast extraction and complete structural drying are the best defense. If mold has already started, our mold remediation after water damage service addresses colonized areas alongside your sewage cleanup.

FAQ

Sewage Backup Cleanup Questions, Answered

What is Category 3 water?
Category 3 water, also called black water, is grossly contaminated water containing bacteria, viruses, parasites, and other pathogens, most commonly from sewage backups, toilet overflows involving waste, or floodwater. It requires biohazard level cleanup procedures under IICRC S500 standards.
Is sewage backup dangerous?
Yes. Raw sewage carries pathogens such as E. coli, Salmonella, Norovirus, and Hepatitis A that can cause serious illness through direct contact, airborne particles, or cross contamination. Professional cleanup with proper PPE and containment is required.
Can I clean sewage myself?
DIY sewage cleanup is not recommended. Without proper PPE, containment, and antimicrobial treatment, homeowners risk exposure to harmful pathogens and often miss hidden moisture that leads to mold growth within 24 to 48 hours.
Does homeowners insurance cover sewage backup cleanup?
Sewage backup is often excluded from standard homeowners policies unless you carry a sewer backup endorsement or rider. We help document damage with moisture readings and photos and coordinate directly with your adjuster either way.
How long does sewage cleanup take?
Most sewage backup cleanups take 3 to 5 days for extraction, contaminated material removal, and structural drying, though larger or more contaminated jobs can take longer.
What must be thrown away after a sewage backup?
Porous materials that absorb sewage, including carpet and padding, drywall, insulation, upholstered furniture, particle board, and cardboard or paper goods, typically cannot be safely sanitized and must be removed.
Can sewage damage cause mold?
Yes. Mold can begin colonizing within 24 to 48 hours after a sewage backup if moisture is not fully extracted and structurally dried, especially inside walls, subfloors, and wet insulation.
How much does sewage backup cleanup cost in Kenosha?
Sewage backup cleanup in Kenosha typically costs more than clean water restoration due to required biohazard protocols and material disposal, often ranging from about $1,500 to $10,000 or more depending on contamination extent. See our full cost breakdown for details.
